# The Reach — lore bible

> The world-spine for Space Traders. Written 2026-09-01 from four forks pinned
> with the user: the Precursors **became the signal**; the setting is a
> **charter reach gone feral**; the MVP planet names are **canonized as charter
> designations**; this doc is the deliverable future content draws from.
>
> Rule zero: **every lore fact pays gameplay rent.** Each section ends with
> *Rent:* — what the fact is FOR (a glyph, a quest rail, a mechanic's meaning).
> Lore that pays no rent gets cut, not written.

---

## 1. The spine — two departures, one song

**The first departure (deep past).** The builders — nobody knows their name,
so the reach calls them the Precursors — did not die and did not leave.
They **transcribed themselves into resonance**: sang themselves into their
own machines and became the signal. Every "dead" relic that still hums, every
relay antenna that still sings, every ferrovolt seam that rings when a drill
bites — those are fragments of them, still transmitting. Not ghosts.
Broadcast. The Void Choir believes the song is alive and listening back.
The unsettling part is that they are *half right* — and no one, including
this document, will ever say which half.

**The second departure (living memory — generations back).** The **Meridian
Charter Combine** — a survey-and-extraction megacorp from somewhere the
charts no longer show — settled this reach for the ferrovolt. It named its
holdings like line items in a ledger, built seven anchor worlds, strung
relays, seeded crops, grew shipminds from patterns it dug out of the
Ossuary. Then, one season, **the jump-freighters stopped coming.** No
announcement. No evacuation. The Withdrawal is the reach's second great
mystery: bankruptcy, quarantine, or something the Combine found in the dig
and fled from — every station bar has a theory and every theory contradicts
the others.

**Now.** Everyone in the reach is descended from the stranded. There is no
navy, no government — just guilds, cartels, and whatever a station can
enforce at its own docks. The reach runs on cannibalized Combine surplus,
DIY tech culture, and nerve. This is why ships are personal, why the
mechanic's bench matters, why every hull is patched: **nothing is new here.
Everything is inherited, salvaged, or grown.**

*Rent:* the mystery is the quest-system's endgame rail (never fully paid
off); the Withdrawal explains the economy (scarcity, salvage, first-come
caches); "no navy" explains why the player does their own bounty work.

## 2. The singing motif — the reach's one aesthetic law

Precursor technology is **resonant**. It hums, rings, sings, broadcasts.
This was already true in five independent MVP flavor lines (the ferrovolt
seams sing, the Drowned Choir's antennae sing, the Wraith Cache hums, the
Silent Beacon pulses, the Songbird mod) — it is now law:

- Anything precursor-touched is described in **sound** words: hum, ring,
  chord, chorus, dirge, static, silence.
- **Silence is significant.** A precursor thing that has gone quiet is
  either dead-dead or holding its breath. Both are story.
- Human tech is described in **mechanical** words: rattle, grind, spark,
  weld. A human machine that starts *singing* has been touched by
  something it shouldn't have.
- Tone check: **family-frontier eerie.** Wonder first, Twilight-Zone hum,
  no gore, no despair. The dark is deep but the campfire is warm.

*Rent:* a one-word style guide for every future blurb, POI, mod name, and
quest line; an audio-design north star if the game ever gets sound.

## 3. Naming — the charter convention and the earned name

Combine designations are **Function + Class + Index**: Agricon Prime,
Mining Station 7, Tech Hub Alpha, Core World Central, Frontier Outpost.
They are official, they are on every chart, and they are *canon* — the
spreadsheet names are a scar the Withdrawal left on the map.

But locals rename places **after something happens there**. An earned name
is a concrete image with a mood: *Ossuary Drift* (they found the tomb-field),
*Meridian Deep* (the Combine's namesake office, drowned in its own
storm-world). The reach's map is a mix of ledger rows and poetry, and the
ratio measures how much history a place has survived.

- "Mining Station **7**" implies Stations 1–6. Their fates are unwritten
  content: collapsed, gone dark, gone feral, or never found. Do not write
  them until a quest needs one.
- "Tech Hub **Alpha**" implies a Beta. Same rule.
- **A planet earning its real name is a live world event** — chronicled,
  broadcast, permanent. The mechanic exists the day we want it: it is a
  rename plus a chronicle entry.

*Rent:* zero code churn now (names stay); a future family-visible world
event ("Mining Station 7 is now the Drum"); station-bar nicknames give
blurbs and dialogue free texture.

## 4. The seven worlds — charter ledger + local truth

Format: **Designation** · nickname · type · faction shadow · character.

**Agricon Prime** · "the Lantern" · agricultural · —
Terraced glowgrain paddies light the nightside like a second aurora. The
Combine's breadbasket concern; the paddies still follow survey-grid lines
you can see from orbit. Gentlest world in the reach; exports calories,
imports everything clever.

**Mining Station 7** · "the Drum" · industrial · **Rustfang Cartel**
A drum of scaffold and dust around a ferrovolt lode. The seams sing when
the drills bite — the miners wear dampeners and pretend they don't hear
words in it. Hit hardest by the Withdrawal: the Combine left mid-shift,
lifts full, wages unpaid. The Rustfang are what the abandoned crews became.

**Tech Hub Alpha** · "the Dreamworks" · technology · —
Orbital foundries where cognition cores dream themselves into being. The
Combine's crown jewel: shipminds grown from seed-patterns dug out of the
Ossuary and never fully understood, then commercialized anyway. The
foundry-guilds guard the seed vaults and do not discuss where patterns
come from. The Void Choir calls every core a caged voice.

**Frontier Outpost** · "Lastlight" · frontier · —
Last dock before the dark. No customs, no questions, no refunds. Built as
the Combine's edge-of-survey camp and never demobilized; now the reach's
pressure valve — the only port where voidbloom trades openly.

**Core World Central** · "the Ledger" · core · —
Old money, high towers, and an appetite for everything the rim digs up.
The Combine's administrative seat; the families who ran the books simply
kept them when the freighters stopped. What passes for law in the reach is
notarized here, which is not the same as enforced.

**Meridian Deep** · "the Deep" · ocean · **Iron Shoal**
A storm-wracked ocean world, the Combine's namesake office drowned under
its own weather. Its reef-labs culture the reach's medicine; the **Meridian
Guild** — the medical division gone native — runs them, and displaced
harvest crews who lost their reefs to Guild consolidation became the Iron
Shoal. Every Shoal boss name is a drowning word.

**Ossuary Drift** · (name already earned) · ruins · **Void Choir** (drawn to it)
A shattered precursor necropolis; the first place in the reach that forced
the charts to tell the truth. The Combine dug here — cognition seed-patterns
came from this dig — and here is where every Withdrawal theory points.
Relic-diggers pay well to stay alive; the Choir circles it like gulls.

*Rent:* faction shadows make grudges and occupations geographic ("the
Rustfang dug in" now means something); nicknames are free blurb/dialogue
texture; planet insignia (a later glyph slice) derive from Combine survey
sigils — one drawn mark per world, spreadsheet-official, locally defaced.

## 5. The factions — every cartel is a planet's shadow

**Rustfang Cartel** — orange, scrap and teeth (Gnash, Korrode, Scrapjaw,
Old Tetanus). The Drum's abandoned shift crews, three generations feral.
They raid freight because in their telling the reach still owes them back
pay. Cartel etiquette: they hit cargo, not crews — a Rustfang boss who
spaces a pilot loses face.

**Void Choir** — purple, liturgical (Choirmasters Dirge, Hymnal,
Echo-of-Nine, Vesper). Began as Combine signal-survey techs stationed at
the Drowned Choir relay. They listened too long; now they believe the song
is the builders, alive in broadcast, and that every cognition core is a
caged fragment of the choir. They raid for relics and shipminds — never
for money — and they are the reach's best argument that the mystery is
real, because *something* answers them. Occasionally. Half right.

**Iron Shoal** — blue-grey, drowning words (Undertow, Riptide, Brack,
Deepmaw). Meridian Deep's dispossessed harvest crews. Amphibious-raised,
they fly like divers — patient, then sudden. They want their reefs back;
the Guild pretends not to remember whose reefs they were.

**Meridian Guild** — the "lawful" remnant: the Combine's medical division
gone native. Not benevolent — just the gang whose hostage is healthcare.
They trade mercy at Guild prices and are owed favors on every world, which
is a currency no cartel can raid. Friendly reputation content lives here
when the rep system grows a positive side.

*Rent:* grudge-weighted occupations gain narrative gravity (the Choir
occupying a precursor site is a *pilgrimage*; Rustfang occupying a cache is
a *wage claim*); faction marks join the glyph language; each faction's
"what they actually want" is a quest-chain seed; VENDETTA-forgiveness (the
backlogged amnesty mechanic) can be faction-flavored — the Choir forgives
those who return a core.

## 6. The goods — eight materials, eight fates

Each good: what it is, why it exists, and its production-chain destiny
(per docs/ship-design-vision.md §5 — goods become ship components when the
modding milestone lands). Glyphs are placeholder-canon: the drawing may
change, the *identity* below is fixed.

- **Glowgrain** (food, yellow) — the Combine's engineered staple; it
  banked light all day and pays it out all night. The Lantern's aurora is
  a crop. *Destiny:* life support, crew rations, morale.
- **Cognition Cores** (technology, cyan) — shipmind lattices grown at the
  Dreamworks from Ossuary seed-patterns. Every core hums one note,
  constantly, just below hearing. *Destiny:* droids, targeting, autopilot
  — the thinking components.
- **Ferrovolt Ore** (materials, orange) — charge-bearing iron, warm to the
  touch; the seams grew along the signal's standing waves, which is the
  polite way of saying the ore is fossilized song. The reason the Combine
  came. *Destiny:* reactors, shields, capacitors — the power components.
- **Nebula Silk** (luxury, magenta) — polymer strands gas-harvested by
  ramscoop clippers and woven at the Ledger into the only new-made luxury
  the reach has. Wealth you can wear. *Destiny:* trade prestige, faction
  gifts, captain's-quarters fittings.
- **Panacea Vials** (medicine, green) — reef-lab cultured cure-alls from
  the Deep. The Guild's monopoly and its mercy; a vial glows faintly, like
  the reef it came from. *Destiny:* med bay, bacta slots, crew revival.
- **Precursor Relics** (relics, violet) — fragments of the builders, still
  transmitting, dug from the Ossuary at famous cost. Lawful ports assay
  them; the Ledger's collectors covet them; the Choir will board you for
  them. *Destiny:* exotic tech — the components with tradeoffs nobody
  fully understands.
- **Voidbloom** (contraband, pink) — a psychoactive flower that grows only
  where the signal is strong; users say a petal lets you *hear the song*
  for an hour. Banned at lawful ports — officially a health rule,
  actually because half of every Choir crew started with one petal.
  *Destiny:* stays contraband — risk cargo, faction currency.
- **Repair Kits** (parts, teal) — sealed Combine surplus, stenciled with a
  defunct logo, guaranteed by a company that no longer exists. The reach
  runs on them. *Destiny:* field repair (already live — R key), hull
  components.

*Rent:* every glyph now depicts something specific (redraw guidance when
the art pass comes); the production chain has lore before it has code;
voidbloom↔Choir ties contraband risk to a faction instead of an abstract
"illegal" flag; market events can be written as story ("the Deep's reefs
bloomed early").

## 7. The seven sites — how the POIs join the spine

- **The Wraith Cache** — an ore-hauler that went silent *during the
  Withdrawal*, holds full. The hull still hums where the crew should be.
  First proof the player meets that the song does something.
- **The Silent Beacon** — a Combine distress channel still pulsing, ages
  after whoever set it stopped waiting. Nobody came then. You came now.
- **Halgren's Eddy** — a gravitational fold where a star should be. On a
  standing-wave node of the signal; instruments sing quietly here.
  (Natural or built? Unwritten — on purpose.)
- **The Ossuary Dig** — the tomb-field the Drift's diggers only whisper
  about; where the Combine's last survey season ended. The dig-quest lives
  here when the quest system lands (questSeed already on the rail).
- **Smuggler's Reef** — a shanty free-port bolted to an asteroid reef;
  the feral economy's id. No song here — which the regulars consider the
  amenity they pay for.
- **The Drowned Choir** — the precursor relay-hulk, antennae still singing
  to nobody. The Void Choir's holy site; their occupations of other POIs
  are attempts to *relay the song* somewhere.
- **The Twin Pulsar** — two dead stars strobing the void white. The reach
  argues about whether it is the loudest singer or just weather. Both
  factions of that bar argument are wrong in interesting ways.

*Rent:* each site's charted/salvage/occupation states (already live) now
carry meaning; three sites (Cache, Beacon, Dig) are Withdrawal-mystery
breadcrumbs for future quest chains.

## 8. Writing rules for future content

1. **Sound for precursor, mechanics for human** (§2). One sentence of
   flavor max per surface — the game shows, blurbs hint.
2. **Never answer the two mysteries** (what the song wants; why the
   Combine left). Quests may *narrow* them. Every answer must open a
   better question.
3. **Names:** charter designations for official things (Function + Class +
   Index); earned names are a concrete image + a mood, granted only after
   something happens in play or in a quest.
4. **Factions want things, not violence.** Every encounter is somebody's
   errand: wages, reefs, voices. Write the errand first.
5. **Family tone:** eerie is a hum in the next room, never a body. Danger
   is real (deaths are real in this game); despair is not.
6. **Rent or cut.** New lore states what mechanic, glyph, quest, or event
   it feeds, or it doesn't go in this file.
7. **No empires. Ever.** Power in the reach is local, personal, and named
   after a person or a grievance — a compound, a crew, a guild, a debt.
   Nothing lore-side may introduce a fleet, a senate, or a chosen one.
   The player is a freelancer making their way through other people's
   errands, and endgame power means *your name in the chronicle*, not a
   throne. (Tone anchor: Mad Max in space — the Combine was the oil
   company, the reach is the junkyard, and the gasoline is ghosts.)

## 9. Player factions (the MMO north star)

When the multiplayer world is ready for it, **players can found their own
faction** — and the lore already dictates how, via rule 4: a faction is
not a flag, it is a *want*. Founding one means declaring the errand
(what does your crew want that the reach owes it?), and the founding is a
naming-event: chronicled, broadcast, permanent — the same machinery that
lets a planet earn its name lets a family crew enter world history.

The seams already exist in code, unplanned but real: grudges are keyed by
faction name as plain data, occupations reference factions as data
(combat-core takes faction maps passed in, never globals), and the
chronicle is the reach's social ladder. A player faction is ultimately a
row in the same table the Rustfang live in — with a human grievance
instead of an authored one.

*Rent:* the MMO milestone's design seed; keeps every faction-system slice
between now and then honest (never hardcode the three authored cartels as
the only possible factions).

## 10. Open hooks (deliberately unwritten)

- Mining Stations 1–6 and Tech Hub Beta — fates unassigned.
- What the Combine's last survey season found in the Ossuary Dig.
- Whether anything is left wherever the Combine withdrew *to*.
- The Meridian Guild's founding bargain (what did the medical division
  trade to survive the Withdrawal intact?).
- What answers the Choir. Occasionally.
- Whether Smuggler's Reef's silence is natural shielding or something
  holding its breath.
